## Build

## Proof

A browser test connects and exchanges frames.

## Built

- `crates/comm/ws` (`orion-ws`): `WsListener` (bind, `protocol`, `run`), `WsSocket` with
  `WsRead::recv` and `WsWrite::send_binary`/`send_text`/`close`, `connect` for the other end,
  and `WsCarrier` for the subprotocol `orion.v1`. The upgrade, the framing, SHA-1 and base64
  are written here: it depends on tokio and nothing else outside the workspace.
- `orion-comm-app`: feature `ws`, on by default, with `ws.listen` and `ws.origins`.

## Proved by

- `tests/browser.rs`: **a real browser.** A headless Firefox loads a page from a small server
  in the test. The page opens `ws://…/orion` with subprotocol `orion.v1`, from an origin the
  comm core was told is the one that may; sends a hello frame; is welcomed; asks who the
  board is; reads the answer; and reports "kinds=2.8&protocol=orion.v1&board=yes". Chromium
  is tried if there is no Firefox. With no browser at all the test says so and passes.
- `tests/ws.rs`, without a browser:
  - Hello, welcome, a request and its answer over a WebSocket; a message in three pieces
    with a ping between them; a goodbye answered.
  - The upgrade: a page request is `426`, not HTTP `400`, an unknown subprotocol `404`,
    another origin `403`; the right origin and no origin are taken; of several subprotocols
    offered the first one spoken here is chosen; the accept key is the standard's example;
    a silent connection and an endless request are dropped at the handshake time.
  - An unmasked client, text, a message of 2^40 bytes and a continuation of nothing each end
    the connection, and the next good one is still served.
  - A second subprotocol on the same listener echoes text and bytes of every length a frame
    can have (0, 125, 126, 65535, 65536) while `orion.v1` goes on beside it.
- Unit tests: SHA-1 against the published vectors including a million bytes, base64 against
  RFC 4648's, and the accept key against RFC 6455's.

## Different from the brief and from dev

- "Read on dev: `crates/nt4/src/net.rs`": dev's WebSocket was inside its NT4 crate, on
  tungstenite. Here the listener is its own crate with no WebSocket library, and NT4 (F7)
  will take its subprotocol from it.
- No TLS. The design's open question stands: a page served over HTTPS cannot open `ws://`.
  A product that needs it puts the comm core behind what serves its pages.
