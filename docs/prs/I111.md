# I111: Build targets in subvolumes of their own; no library compiled to run no tests

**Status:** done

**Group:** I. Finish

**Wave:** 19

**Needs:** I110

Read `AGENTS.md` and `docs/design.md` first.

## Why

The one-minute rule (2026-11-24). After I110 a change to a core crate still took 550 s.
Rebuilding the tests took 479 s of that, on 398 CPU-s.

- **The builds waited on the filesystem.** Every thread of a core change's test build was
  sampled (`scripts/wait-states.py`). rustc was in uninterruptible wait four to five times as
  often as it ran: on `btrfs_inode_lock`, `getdents64`, `filename_create` and
  `btrfs_tree_read_lock`. Its target shared one btrfs tree with every other build on the disk.
  Its `debug/deps` held 40,906 entries, and rustc lists that directory on every compile.
- **The build is throughput-bound, not chain-bound.** All 13 job slots were busy throughout,
  with a median of 74 units ready and waiting for one. The chain of crates that must build in
  turn is about 22 s. What costs is the number and size of the units: 258, 164 of them test
  targets.
- **49 libraries were compiled a second time, for their unit tests, and had none.** That was 7%
  of the build's unit time, run for nothing.

## Changes

- **`scripts/work-area.sh`** makes this machine's work area: worktrees, targets, caches and
  logs. The target root, and the size gate's and fuzzing's targets in it, are each a btrfs
  subvolume of their own. It makes them without root and without a mount change; `--check` says
  what is missing. The target was rebuilt fresh in one, never moved in.
- **The fuzz targets build in a directory of their own** (`target/fuzz`), not beside the
  tests in the target root.
- **`[lib] test = false` on the 49 libraries with no unit test.** `scripts/workspace.py
  lib-tests`, a gate step, fails both ways: a library with no unit test still built for them,
  and one marked `test = false` that has a test, which would never run. Doctests are not
  affected.
- **`scripts/wait-states.py`** runs a command and says where its threads spent their time:
  running, waiting for a processor, or in uninterruptible wait, and on what.
  **`scripts/ab-target.sh`** compares build targets on a core change, in rotated order, under
  the same load.

## Proved by

- **`lib-tests`**, on `orion-values`. With a unit test added, 3 of 3 failed, saying it would
  never run. With `test = false` removed, 3 of 3 failed, saying it is built for nothing.
- **The targets** (`scripts/ab-target.sh 2`): a comment added to `orion-types` and taken out
  again, every test rebuilt, 4 builds each, order rotated, load 91 to 192.

  | Target | Median wall | Range | CPU-s |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | as it was, on the shared tree | 178 s | 73 to 408 s | 143 |
  | the same files, in a subvolume | 66 s | 42 to 127 s | 137 |
  | a fresh subvolume (1,264 entries in `deps`) | 46 s | 36 to 66 s | 134 |

  The CPU time is the same; the waits are not. Uninterruptible waits were 10 to 11% of thread
  samples on the shared tree and 5 to 6% in the fresh subvolume. The tree's lock waits
  (`btrfs_tree_read_lock_nested`, `btrfs_inode_lock`) fell from about 120 samples a build to
  about 10.

## Measured

Filled in at landing: the leaf and core changes' gates again, with the load each ran under.
