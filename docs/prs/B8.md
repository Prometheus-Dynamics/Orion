## Build

- Each reader marks what it holds in a page only that reader writes. A buffer is free when no reader marks it.
- A dead reader's marks go away with its page.
- Limits on frames held and on hold time. A reader over a limit gets no more, and a stuck one is cut off.
- The publisher never waits for buffers. It drops frames instead.

Fixed after it was merged, found while building C5: the publisher counted a reader's marks one
word at a time, so a reader swapping one buffer for another could be seen over its limit and cut
off, and its held frames were then written over. The marks page now starts with a version word and
the count is taken at one instant. A first attempt at the rule for marks that cannot be counted
(cut off after 32 failed looks) was wrong too: a reader thread descheduled mid-change failed it
at once. It is judged by time now. `docs/ring.md` has the rule.

"A dead reader's marks go away with its page" holds once the publisher drops its own mapping of
that page, which it does when the comm core tells it the reader is gone. Until then the hold-time
limit covers a dead reader the same as a stuck one. Both are tested with a reader process that is
killed while holding a frame.

## Proof

Stuck, slow and killed readers never starve the others. 0 allocations.
