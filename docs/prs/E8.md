## Built

`crates/node/sync` (`orion-sync`): `Sync` with `peer_up`, `peer_down`, `receive`, `changed`,
`resync`, `tick`, `next_deadline`, `status`, `forgive`, `agreed_at` and `restore_agreed`. It
uses the two peer kinds as they were recorded; no kind was added. `Store::observed_slice` was
added to E1's store. The rules are in `docs/node.md`, "Peer sync".

## Proved by

`tests/converge.rs`: three stores and three syncs joined by a network that carries the encoded
messages and can be cut between any two nodes.

- A is cut from B and C. Each side writes; the same record is written on both sides; a record
  is deleted on one side and rewritten on the other, and the reverse. Reconnected, all three
  hold the same records, the later write won each time, and every pair reports `InSync`.
- 300 seeded histories of 120 steps each (writes, deletes, cuts, reconnects, partial
  delivery) all end with the three stores identical and every pair in sync. With "send
  everything" made to send nothing, this and four other tests fail.
- A difference no stamp reveals (an old record on one side, a newer newest on the other) is
  found by the digest, with everything sent at most once.
- An empty node is filled with 500 records in several messages without anything being sent
  whole; a node between two that never connect passes writes both ways; a version is not sent
  back to the peer it came from; nodes at rest send nothing.
- A record stamped ten minutes ahead is refused, sync comes to rest in under 40 messages, each
  later write costs under 12 and no further full send, and the record is taken once time has
  caught up.
- Observed slices reach direct peers only, follow changes, and are dropped when the peer goes.
- A message under another node's name is `Denied`; a stale peer is not synced with either way
  until forgiven; a pair that stayed connected is not stale.

## Different from dev and from the brief

- Dev synced in rounds on a timer, with hello, summary, diff, snapshot and mutation-batch
  requests over its own TCP protocol, revisions and a mutation history. The wire here has one
  body for all of it, so the protocol is the four rules above and is driven by events alone.
- Dev's sections (workloads, artifacts, providers, executors, update intents) are gone with
  its control plane: sync carries whatever classes the store holds.
- Not built here: the connections. Peers reach node logic through the comm core, and what
  dials them is the comm core's outbound part, which does not exist yet. `orion-sync` is
  ready for it: `peer_up` and `peer_down` when a peer session comes and goes, `receive` for
  its frames, `changed` after every `take_changes`. That wiring, the relay routes for the two
  peer kinds, and keeping `agreed_at` in the state directory are E18's.
