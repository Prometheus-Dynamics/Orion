## Build

- Read, write and watch records. Watches are push.

## Proof

Watch delivers every change in order.

## Built

- `orion-records`: `Records::put`, `get` and `watch`. A `Watch` gives each change with `poll`
  or `wait`, and `newest` is the stamp to watch from next time.
- Watches are pushed: after the watch request the client asks for nothing. A watch starts with
  everything newer than a stamp, or everything there is.
- A watcher's queue is bounded (1,024 changes). When it fills, the watch stops taking changes
  and, once the program has read what it holds, asks the store for everything newer than the
  last change it was given.

## Proved by

`tests/records.rs`, against a store written in the test: 500 changes to two records, with as
many changes to a third record in between, arrive in the order they were made with rising
stamps and none from the third. A watch from a kept stamp sees only what it missed. A watcher
that reads nothing through 3,072 changes then reads all 3,072 records once each, in stamp order.

## For E (the store)

Two things this client expects of the store, which the wire's comments did not say:

- A `Put` is answered with `Changed`, naming the `Put`'s `seq`, carrying the records as stored
  with the stamps they were given.
- A `Watch` from a session that already watches the same class and prefix replaces the older
  one, and its pushes name the newer `seq`.

## Not carried from dev

Dev's client kept a local view of every record and rebuilt it by polling. There is no view
here: a program that wants one keeps what its watch gives it.
