## Creates

`web/video-example`

## Build

- JPEG chunks and H.264 through Media Source, both on plain HTTP.
- Orion moves bytes and only the page knows the format.

## Proof

Plays in a headless browser, on `http://`, with a throttled link.

## Built

- `web/video-example`: `video.js` (`showJpeg` into an `<img>`, `playH264` into a `<video>`
  through Media Source in sequence mode), `index.html`, a README, and `media/`: a short test
  clip as 16 JPEG pictures and as fragmented MP4 (one fragment a picture, groups of eight).
- `orion-web-tests`: `video::publish` plays the clip in a loop on `camera.jpeg` and
  `camera.h264` (example `publish-video`). A fragment that starts a group is published as
  "others depend on this one" and begins with the stream's header, so a page can begin at
  any of them. The crate's browser and site helpers are shared with F4's test.
- `orion-ws`: `WsOptions::send_buffer` (`ws.send_buffer`, 256 KiB) caps what the kernel
  holds for one browser. See below.

## Proved by

`cargo test -p orion-web-tests --test video`:

- A headless browser loads the page over `http://` from an address that is not the
  loopback, so the page is not a secure context and has no WebCodecs. The link to the board
  carries 48 KiB a second; the camera publishes about three times that.
- JPEG pictures are shown at their size; H.264 frames are decoded and the play head moves.
- Both topics lose samples to the link, and H.264 plays at least 24 frames more after its
  loss: the next fragment the board sent was a key frame.
- The clip is cut at its fragments, key frames carry the header, and junk is not a clip.

A browser without an H.264 decoder is reported and only JPEG is required. If no browser is
installed the test prints that and passes.

## Different from the brief

- F3 is changed: the WebSocket listener caps the kernel's send buffer. Without it the first
  run of this proof dropped nothing in a minute: the kernel took megabytes, the page showed
  video twenty seconds old, and `outbound.max_bytes` never came into it. The cap makes the
  outbound limit mean what it says for a browser.
- The publisher is an example of the test crate, not part of `web/video-example`: the page
  is the example, and the clip is test media.
