## Proof

Builds for every MCU target, within the size budget.

## Built

- `crates/device/core` (`orion-device-core`): `no_std`, no alloc. `Link<TX>` is the session:
  hello at a fixed pace until answered, welcome, a ping at once and then every heartbeat, pongs
  owed, the host taken for gone after missed heartbeats with hello again in the same poll, and a
  wait only after a reject. `StreamDevice` (COBS) and `CanDevice` (classic and FD segments) put
  it on a carrier. `Link::send_with(kind, write)` takes one frame at a time; the session's own
  frames have their own buffer and go first. The millisecond counter may wrap.
- `codec`: a small `Writer`/`Reader` that writes and reads postcard's format by hand.
- `crates/device/probe` (`orion-device-probe`): the smallest firmware that is one, three C entry
  points, used only to measure.
- `scripts/device-size.sh`, in the gate: links the probe per target and variant with
  `--gc-sections` from its entry points and holds flash and RAM to a budget.

## Proved by

- `crates/device/core/tests/session.rs`: hello pace; a session that pings, answers pings and
  carries frames; the host's frame limit; a host going quiet; reject backoff; a wrapping counter;
  the same session over CAN classic and FD; no allocation while running (counting allocator).
  The host side of every test encodes with postcard, so the hand-written codec is checked against
  it.
- `scripts/device-size.sh` builds for thumbv6m, thumbv7em, riscv32imc and riscv32imac. Measured
  flash/RAM, stream and CAN: v6m 6262/512 and 6438/544, v7em 5758/512 and 5962/544, riscv
  6742/512 and 6840/544.

## Different from the brief

- The budget is per target and per carrier, on a probe linked only from its entry points, rather
  than one number for the STM32G4 example, which is H6's. Dev's 9,228 B was a whole firmware; this
  is the session alone, and it is under that.
- With postcard and serde the probe was 13.1 KB on thumbv7em. Most of that was `core::fmt` and
  panic paths reached through them, so the device path encodes its own frames by hand.
