## Build

A lease with no end is never freed by time. A program on the board that holds one and then stops
answering (stopped in a debugger, wedged) keeps its session, since C37, and so keeps the lease,
and nothing else can use the resource. This lets another claimant take it once the holder has
been quiet long enough, tells the old holder who took it when it speaks again, and tells a
claimant that is refused who holds it and since when.

## Built

**The comm core tells node logic when a session is quiet.**

- `Service::quiet(session, quiet_ms, sessions)`: every service is told when a program on the
  board's socket goes quiet past the idle time (with how long it has been quiet), and when it is
  heard again (zero).
- The relay passes it to node logic as `NodeQuiet { session, quiet_ms }`, kind `0xA0`, for a
  session it was passed a request from. The session range `0x00`–`0x0F` is full, so this opens
  a new range, `Node`, `0xA0`–`0xAF`. It is recorded in `vectors.txt`.

**Leases (`orion-resources`, `contest.rs`).**

- Node logic keeps who wrote each lease, which sessions are quiet and since when (on its own
  clock), and what each session lost.
- A claim refused as busy is a contest when all of these hold:
  - the lease has no end;
  - it was written by another session here;
  - that session has been quiet for `leases.quiet_ms` (default 10000);
  - that session has no run of one of the resource's actions under way.
  Then the claim succeeds, it is logged at warn, and the old session is noted as having lost it.
- Otherwise the refusal is `busy` with words, for example "cam.front is held by
  workload:detector since 1794685315075 ms: its holder is answering". The other reasons: "quiet
  N ms: a lease is taken only after 10000 ms quiet", "a run of its action is under way", "its
  lease ends on its own", "its holder is not a session here".
- The old holder's writes and frees of that lease, and its runs of the resource's actions, are
  refused `LeaseLost` (22) with "lease lost while quiet: taken by <holder>", while the taker
  holds it. After the taker lets go, the old holder may claim it like anyone.
- Nothing is lost without a claimant. A session that is gone, or forgotten by a restart, holds
  nothing to contest and has lost nothing.

**Words on node logic's refusals.** `Out::refuse_with(asked, error, detail)`. Records keeps the
words of its last refusal for `Logic::request` to send.

**Levels.** `Error::LeaseLost = 22`, level 2 (`LEASE_LOST_LEVEL`). A session below level 2 is
told `busy`. Code 21 stays reserved for J9's layout refusal.

**The wrapper class is closed.** Building this found that two wrappers did not pass the new
behaviour on:

- the comm core's own carrier list (`comm-app`'s `Connections`) did not pass `local()` on. So in
  a real comm core a program on the socket was still dropped for being quiet: a C37 defect that
  C37's tests, on the server and unix crates directly, could not see;
- the device gateway's `Watch<S>`, which wraps the relay, did not pass `quiet` on.

`Connection::local` and `Service::quiet` now have no default body, so every implementation says
what it does and the compiler finds the next wrapper. These are the only wrappers there are:

- `Connection`: `comm-app` `Connections` (passes it on).
- `Service`: `device-gateway` `Watch<S>` (passes it on).

Leaves: Unix (true), Mem (as built), TCP, serial, CAN and WebSocket (false). Services with nothing
to do about quiet have an empty body.

## Proved by

- `resources/tests/contest.rs`, 6 tests:
  - a quiet holder loses to a claimant and is refused with whom for its claim, its free and its
    action, but not for another action or another session; once the taker lets go it may claim
    again;
  - a holder that answers, has not been quiet long enough, or was heard again keeps its lease,
    and the words say who, since when and why;
  - a run of the resource's action that the holder asked for blocks a claim; another action, or
    another session's run, does not;
  - no claimant: nothing lost after a thousand quiet times, and the holder renews;
  - a lease with an end is not contested;
  - gone and forgotten sessions.
- `node-app/tests/leases.rs`, end to end through a comm core and node logic:
  - a program holds the camera and stops pumping its session;
  - a claim is refused with who and since when;
  - after `leases.quiet_ms` the claim succeeds, not before;
  - the old holder's claim and its run of `cam.front.focus` are refused 22 with "taken by
    workload:recorder".
- `comm-app/tests/comm.rs`: a program on the real comm core's socket says nothing for 2.5 s,
  against a 1 s idle time, and is still answered. It fails without the `local()` forwarding.
- `server/tests/drops.rs`: services are told once of the quiet (with the idle time) and once
  of the heard-again.
- `spoken`: 22 below level 2 is `busy`. Codes pinned.
- 24 mutations, 24 caught (c38-mut.py).
- The node-app suite was run 6 times.

## Different from the brief

- "When the old holder speaks again it is told": it is told on its next request under the lease,
  as the refusal of that request. There is no unasked-for message, because no kind carries one.
  A program watching leases also sees the lease change.
- A refused claimant learns "since when" as the lease's stamp (wall-clock ms of the write that
  holds it).
- A lease freed by anyone but its writer was, and still is, allowed for any session except one
  that lost it. Narrowing that is not in this brief; it is on the PARKED list.
