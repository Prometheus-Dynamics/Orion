## Build

## Proof

A sample executor runs against a stub.

## Built

- `orion-executor`: the `Executor` trait as the design gives it (`kinds`, `start`, `stop`,
  `kill`, `inventory`) and `Serving`, which runs one on a session. `Serving::start` sends the
  offer and the whole inventory; `Serving::turn` does what Orion asked and reports every
  workload whose state changed.
- The executor never reports by hand. Each turn its inventory is compared with what Orion was
  last told: a changed state is reported, and a workload no longer in the inventory is reported
  gone. A start that fails is reported failed with the reason.
- After a reconnect, `start` again sends the inventory, which is how Orion takes back runs that
  carried on without it.

## Proved by

`tests/executor.rs`: a sample executor against a tracker written in the test. A start is
reported `Starting` then `Running`, once each; a spec that does not load is reported `Failed`
with its reason; a stop is `Stopping` then `Gone`; twenty idle turns report nothing. The
tracker is then stopped and started again: the run carries on, and the inventory after the
reconnect names it as running, with no report needed. A kill ends it.

## Different from the brief

The design's `start(name, spec)` has no kind and no CPUs, and `ExecutorStart` carries both.
The trait keeps `start` as designed and adds `start_on(name, kind, spec, cpus)`, which calls
`start` unless an executor overrides it. A-series interfaces are unchanged.
