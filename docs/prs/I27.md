# I27: Every gate step has a bound, and leaves nothing running

**Status:** done

**Group:** I. Finish

**Wave:** 19

**Needs:** I26

Read `AGENTS.md` and `docs/design.md` first.

## Changes

`scripts/bounded.py` (new), `scripts/gate.sh`, `packaging/busybox/orion-supervise`, `AGENTS.md`,
`docs/lock.md`, `TODO.md`.

## Why

Another repository's landing gate hung for 1 h 40 min. A helper started a child, exited under
`set -e` before it signalled the child, and the orphan held the gate's output pipe open, so the
reader of that pipe never saw its end. Nothing in this gate bounded a step either: a hung step
waited as long as whoever ran the gate did.

## Built

- `scripts/bounded.py SECONDS LABEL -- COMMAND...` runs one step:
  - in a session and process group of its own, with /dev/null for input;
  - its output and errors go to a file, copied to the gate's output as it grows. A process the
    step leaves can hold that file, never the gate's pipe;
  - as the step's subreaper: a process the step leaves behind, even one in a session of its
    own, becomes the wrapper's child, so it can be found;
  - past its bound, the step's group and every process under the wrapper get TERM, then KILL
    5 s later. Exit 124, `TIMED OUT: <label>` and the bound;
  - a step that ends with a process of its own still running fails with 125, naming each
    process, and they are ended;
  - TERM, INT or HUP to the wrapper ends the step the same way;
  - `--self-test` plants a sleeper, a process in a session of its own that ignores TERM, an
    orphan that holds the output, and a stop from outside. All at once, 2.4 s.
- `scripts/gate.sh`: every step, in the main part and in each lane, runs under the wrapper.
  After its label is the slowest time it has taken in a gate that passed. Its bound is three
  times that, at least 60 s. A step that is a function of the gate runs in a shell of its own,
  with the functions it calls exported. Waiting for the lanes runs in the gate's own shell,
  where they are its children; each lane step has its own bound. When the gate stops early, it
  waits for each lane's process group to end, at most 15 s, so nothing is left after it exits.
- The step bounds self-test is a gate step.

that run's time.

## A leftover the check found

The packaging check's supervisor test failed under the wrapper with `sleep 4` left running. The
cause is in the shipped BusyBox supervisor (`packaging/busybox/orion-supervise`). It waits
between starts on a `sleep` in the background. A TERM during that wait ran `stop`, which exits
at once when no child is running, and the `sleep` (up to 30 s) was left. `stop` now ends the
wait first. On the old supervisor the devices part fails with exit 125, naming the `sleep`; on
the new one it passes.

## Proof

Each of these ran with the gate's output piped through `cat`, so an orphan holding the pipe
would hang it:

- A planted main step that sleeps: red in 60 s (its bound), `TIMED OUT: planted sleeper`, exit
  124.
- A planted main step that starts a child and exits under `set -e` before it signals it (the
  hang above): red in 1 s, exit 125, naming the orphan.
- A planted lane step that sleeps, with the lane run alone: red at 84 s, the 24 s of the lane's
  steps before it plus its 60 s bound.
- A full gate with every lane building, and a main step failing 20 s in: red at 30 s. Nothing
  of the gate was left running when it exited, checked at once: no process in the worktree,
  none naming its target directory.
- The wrapper's self-test, against three mutations of the wrapper:
  - without the subreaper, the orphan is not found: it fails;
  - with ending processes taken out: it fails, naming what was left;
  - with the step's output going straight to the wrapper's own: it passes. The subreaper ends
    the orphan before the reader could block, so the file only matters for a process that
    cannot be ended (another user's). That cannot be planted without privileges.

## Cost

The wrapper adds about 0.17 s to each step, mostly Python's start, so about 3 s over the main
steps. On a normal end it checks only its own child list. It reads the whole process table only
when that list is not empty. The self-test adds 2.4 s.
