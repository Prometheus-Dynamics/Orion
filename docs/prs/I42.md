## Why

`cargo test -p orion-comm-app --test all`, run in one process, failed
`a_running_comm_core_offers_its_own_files_with_secrets_left_out`: its core offered `orion/log`
bounded at 262144 bytes, not the 4096 its settings said. The gate runs that binary under
nextest, a process per test, and never saw it.

The window's size was the process's: `orion_log::set_window_bytes`, called by each comm core
as it started. Another core starting in the same process, here another test's, changed it
for the first. A program that runs two comm cores has the same bug. A lock around the tests
would have hidden it.

## How

`orion_log::Window::new(bytes)` is a window of its own size on the process's log, with
`bytes()` and `read()`. `read()` gives the newest whole lines within that window's own bytes.
The lines are still the process's, one log whoever wrote them. They are kept for the largest
window open, or `WINDOW_BYTES` while none is, and dropping a window lets its share go.

The comm core's own files hold their `Window`. `orion/log` is bounded by it and read through
it. `set_window_bytes`, `window_bytes` and `window` are gone. No other repository called them.

## Proof

- `two_comm_cores_in_one_process_each_keep_their_own_log_window` starts cores with windows of
  4096 and 262144 bytes in one process and logs more than 4096 bytes. Each core's index bounds
  `orion/log` by its own size. The small one's log is at most 4096 bytes and the large one's
  more, and the small one still has the newest line. With the source from before and this test,
  it fails 3 of 3 (`left: Some(262144)`, `right: Some(4096)`), and passes after.
- orion_log's own test has a small window beside a large one. The small one never reads more
  than its own bytes while the large one keeps more, and a line too long for the small one is
  left out of it whole. Dropping the large one lets the lines go.
- With `read()` planted to give every kept line, both tests fail.
- The running core's test no longer looks for its own line in its 4096-byte `orion/log`. The
  lines are the process's, and other tests in it log too, so which are newest is not that
  test's to say (with the line check in place, one of the first in-process runs failed there).
  It checks the size and that the lines are whole. The lines themselves are looked for
  through the 262144-byte window of the two-core test, the first and the last of its 200.
- The whole comm-app `all` binary passes in one process with `--all-features`, 5 of 5.
