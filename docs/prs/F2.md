## Built

- `crates/comm/outbound` (`orion-outbound`): `Outbound::start(comm, broker, limits)`,
  `OutboundLimits`, `counters`, `subscriptions`. It is what sends samples to a subscriber
  that was not given a ring; until now such a subscriber was told the topic was live and was
  sent nothing.
- `orion-broker`: `Topics::set_offboard_bell`. A subscriber without a ring cannot be rung, so
  the doorbell of what carries its samples is given to publishers in its place.
- `orion-comm-app`: feature `outbound`, on by default, and the setting `outbound.max_bytes`.
- `orion-stubs`: the test board has it, so the client's tests can subscribe over the network.

## Proved by

- `tests/outbound.rs`, a comm core in memory with programs that have no descriptors:
  - **A throttled reader beside a fast one.** 3 MB is published in two fields, one with a
    key every tenth sample. The fast subscriber gets all 3 000 samples in order, and the
    publisher runs at the fast subscriber's pace throughout. The throttled one, which takes
    a sample every 2 ms, gets fewer, in order; it lost samples of both fields; in the keyed
    field the first sample after every loss is a key; and its session is still open.
  - A subscriber that does not read at all: 300 KiB is published in well under two seconds,
    most of it dropped for that subscriber; when it reads again it finds no more waiting
    than its limit, and then gets what is published next.
  - With a reader that keeps up, 80 of 80 samples are sent and none dropped; after it
    unsubscribes nothing more is sent.
- `tests/network.rs`, with the real client and a real comm core: a subscriber over
  `orion+tcp` and one on the board's socket run the same reading function and both get the
  same 200 samples in order, the same latest value, and what is published afterwards.

## Different from dev

Dev pushed topic data to remote clients from the node with a bounded queue a client and
dropped the oldest. There was no notion of a sample others depend on.

## Found on the way

A ring reader sees what is published after it is made. The first version made its readers a
moment after the subscription, on its next turn, and lost the first samples. Readers are now
made inside the broker's own notice of the subscription.
