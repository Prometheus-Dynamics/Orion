# J3: Name the clock

**Status:** done

**Group:** J. The stack around Orion

**Wave:** 17

**Needs:** J1

**Runs:** cloud

Read `AGENTS.md` and `docs/design.md` first.

## Build

- A sample time is nanoseconds of `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` on the board that took the sample: said
  in the type and given by its `now()`.
- Time sync publishes each board's offset between its monotonic and realtime clocks in
  `orion.time`, and topics carried to another board, and the device gateway, convert sample
  times into the receiving board's clock, so a lookup by sample time means the same thing
  everywhere.

## Proof

Two boards with different monotonic clocks: a sample carried from one is found at the right
time on the other.

## Built

- `SampleTime` is nanoseconds of `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` on the board that took the sample, said in
  its docs. `SampleTime::now()` reads it, behind the `now` feature of `orion-types` (rustix,
  no `unsafe`), so the type stays `no_std` for a microcontroller, which stamps from its own
  clock.
- Everything Orion publishes itself stamps with `now()`: node logic's events, `orion.status`,
  `orion.time`, the NT4 gateway and `orion-ctl publish`. Before, they used the wall clock.
- `TimeRequest` says which clock to answer with, `CLOCK_REALTIME` (time sync, the client's
  clock check) or `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` (a topic link). Another is `unsupported`.
- Topic links compare the two boards' monotonic clocks and move each sample's time by the
  difference, as they did before with the wall clocks.
- `orion.time` has `monotonic_to_wall_ns`: added to a sample time on that board it gives the
  wall time. Published again when it moves by more than a millisecond (a slew moves it a
  little all the time).

## Proved by

- `crates/comm/topic-peers/tests/across.rs` `sample_times_arrive_in_this_boards_time`: two
  comm cores whose monotonic clocks are five seconds apart; a sample stamped on one arrives
  on the other at the other's time for the same moment, within 20 ms. Were either side on the
  wall clock, the two would be decades apart.
- `crates/comm/time/tests/time.rs`: a `TimeRequest` for the monotonic clock is answered on it,
  one for the wall clock on that, and another clock is refused.
- The wire vectors carry the new `TimeRequest`.

## Different from the brief

- The device gateway carries records of the device daemon's devices, not samples: there is no
  sample time in it to convert. The device library stamps its own samples; on the same board
  they are on the same monotonic clock as Orion's.
- No run across two real boards: the test shifts one comm core's clock by five seconds instead,
  which is the same code path.
