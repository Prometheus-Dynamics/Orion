## Built

- `crates/device/topics` (`orion-device-topics`), `no_std`, no alloc. `TopicSpec` and `Field`
  are constants a firmware keeps in flash; `Topics<N>` declares each when a session starts (the
  same `TopicDeclare` any publisher sends), says it again after a second without an answer,
  keeps the handle from `Opened`, and publishes `TopicSample` frames: `publish_with` for any
  payload, `publish_f64`, `publish_i64` and `publish_bool` for the fixed ones. `handle` reads
  `Opened`, a `Refusal` of a declaration, and `Demand` with the subscriber's request bytes.
- `Requests<N>` in the device core: ask once a session, again until answered, not after a
  refusal. Topics use it; actions and firmware will.
- `crates/testing/device-host` (`orion-device-host`): a host played by a test that reads and
  writes every body with the general codec, answers pings unless told to go quiet, and drives
  one layer the way a firmware's main loop does.
- The size probe's `topics` variant, with its budgets in `scripts/device-size.sh`.

## Proved by

`crates/device/topics/tests/topics.rs`, against the simulated host: every declaration decodes as
the host reads it; a sample reaches the host once the topic is open and not before; a payload
long enough for a two-byte length and its flags arrive whole, and one too long is refused with
nothing sent; one sample at a time; an unanswered declaration is said again after a second and
an answer to the first saying is not taken for the second; a refused topic waits for the next
session; a new session declares everything again; demand carries the count and the request;
publishing allocates nothing.

## Different from the brief

- The device sends one sample per frame and no `TopicSampleBatch`: a frame is the unit a link
  can lose, and the newest value is what a device should send next, not a backlog.
- A sample's time is the firmware's to give, in nanoseconds on the host's clock. Time sync for
  devices comes with the host side (H5).
