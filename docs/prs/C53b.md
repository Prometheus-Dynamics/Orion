## Build

From the review of C53:

- C53's `tests/alive.rs` connected with the 300 ms timeout its replies were measured against,
  and under the gate's load the handshake took longer. C53 retried the connect, and a retry is
  no fix for a flake. Make it deterministic: no short connect deadline in the test and no
  retry, which means a connect bound that is not the thing under test.
- C53's deviation, only a detached start taking a number, needs the other direction shown safe.
  Session one has a kept detached answer under N; session two starts a plain run as N. Its
  result must be its own, and a bare attach to N afterwards must be refused or give the plain
  run's result, never the old detached answer.

## Built

### A reply bound of its own

`Options::timeout` bounded connecting, the hello and, under `Reply::Timeout`, every answer:
one number for two things, which is what C53's brief asked to split.

- `Reply::Within(bound)`: each answer within `bound`. `Options::timeout` then bounds
  connecting and the hello alone.
- In C, `orion_set_reply(orion, ORION_REPLY_WITHIN, bound_ms)`. A bound of 0 is refused as an
  argument error.
- A push's commit wait under `Within` is that bound, as under `Alive` it is the liveness rule.

**The tests.**

- Every session in `tests/alive.rs` connects with the usual timeout, once, with no retry. The
  test node serves one connection again.
- The default rule is read from `reply_left` itself: the time left is the connect timeout, and
  a wait begun that long ago has had its time. No test waits a timeout out.
- `Within(300 ms)` is measured in a real wait: at least 300 ms, and well short of the
  connect timeout.
- Setting the bound back is shown from `Within`.

### Every run takes its number

Back to the approved design: any run started under a number, detached or not, takes it.

- The starter's earlier runs under it, going or kept, are reached by their handles only.
- A bare attach to the number is refused as `NotFound`, saying to come back by the handle.

In the reviewer's case, a plain run's result is not kept, so the refusal is the answer.

C53 had changed this because its end-to-end test ended its held runs from another session of
the same user, whose plain run 1 then took the dashboard's number. That test now ends them from
session two itself, whose later numbers are past the one in play.

## Proved by

- `orion-connect` `tests/alive.rs`:
  - by default the bound is the timeout, read from `reply_left`;
  - a bound of its own is kept apart from the connect timeout;
  - the bound is kept until it is set again, back to `Within`.
- `orion-capi`: `ORION_REPLY_WITHIN` with a bound is taken, and with 0 is refused.
- `orion-action-list` `tests/handles.rs`,
  `a_plain_run_under_a_kept_number_is_its_own_and_the_old_answer_is_never_given_for_it`:
  - session one's detached answer is kept under 7, and session two runs plain 7;
  - its result is its own;
  - a bare attach to 7 is refused with words, and nothing is sent;
  - the old answer comes by its handle.
- `orion-node-app` `tests/actions.rs`: the end-to-end handle test, under the restored rule.
- Mutations, each killed:
  - a kept run marked only by a detached start (the C53 rule);
  - `Within` reading the connect timeout instead of its bound.

## Different from the brief

- The brief allowed "a connect bound that isn't the thing under test". There was no such
  bound, so this adds `Reply::Within`, the split C53's own brief asked for, in Rust and C. The
  web client has no connect timeout of this kind and is unchanged.
