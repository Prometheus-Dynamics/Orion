## Built

- `orion-topics`: `Topics::declare` gives a `Publisher`, `Topics::subscribe` a `Subscriber`.
  `publish`, `next`, `latest`, `at_or_before` and `wait` are the same calls wherever the comm
  core is.
- On the board a publish is a write to the ring and a ring of each sleeping subscriber's
  doorbell; a read is a memory read. Off the board the same publisher sends `Sample` messages
  for the comm core to write, and the same subscriber keeps a ring of its own for samples that
  arrive as messages.
- `Subscriber::wait` is the pollable wait: it arms the doorbell, looks once more, and sleeps on
  the doorbell and the session together. Nothing is polled on a timer, and the session's pings
  are kept up while it sleeps.
- A subscriber may come before the topic exists and is given the ring when it is declared. It
  is told when its publisher goes and keeps what was published.
- `orion-connect` gained `Client` and `Handler`: one session shared by every part of a client,
  each incoming frame going to the part that registered for its kind. Records, actions and the
  rest will hang off it. `orion-ring` gained `Reader::pending`.

## Proved by

`tests/topics.rs`: one function run with the publisher on the board and again with it across
the network, a program on the board reading both. 0 allocations in 10,000 publishes and in
30,000 reads (`latest`, `next`, `at_or_before`). A subscriber asleep with a 3 s wait is woken by
each of five publishes from another program in well under a second.

## Not here

- **Samples to a subscriber off the board.** Its calls work and it is given the declaration,
  but the comm core does not yet send samples to a subscriber that cannot map the ring. That is
  F2 (outbound limits and the drop rule). The client's side of it is written and has not seen a
  sample.
- **Topics after a reconnect.** A session that is made again does not yet declare and subscribe
  again by itself. The declaration is kept for it; the facade (D9) is where the pieces meet.
