# D16: Files from a board

- **Status:** done
- **Group:** D (files)
- **Wave:** 19
- **Needs:** D15

## Why

A file went to a board, never from one. A tool that collects what explains a board's trouble (its
kernel log, the device library's state, Orion's own) had to know where each lives, and reach it
by SSH. Programs on the board know their own files; Orion should carry them, as it carries a push,
and know nothing of what they hold.

## Changes

- **Wire.** Eight kinds, `FILE_OFFER` (0x27) to `FILE_OFFERS_LIST` (0x2E): `FileOffer` and
  `FileOffered`; `FileFetch` (transfer, name, `from`, and `caller` and `max_chunk`, said by the
  comm core); `FileGive` (size, digest) and `FileGiveChunk`; `FileTaken` (how much the client
  has, and where it is); `FileOffers` and `FileOffersList` (`clashes`, and each prefix with
  `OfferedBy`, name, principal and since). `FETCH_WINDOW` is 8. Level 4, `Feature::FileOffers`,
  and `INTERFACE_LEVEL` is 4.
- **The comm core routes** (`orion-file-offers`, feature `offers`, on by default). The longest
  prefix offered gets the fetch, renumbered for it. The first program to offer a prefix holds it
  while its session lives, and another is refused as busy, naming it, logged and counted. Each
  side is told when the other goes. The chunks asked for fit a window in the client's outbox twice
  over (`Sessions::outbox_bytes`, added).
- **The client.** `Files::offer(prefix)` -> `Offer`, whose `next` hands an `Asked` (name, from,
  caller) to `refuse(why)` or `give(source)` / `give_known(source, Known)` -> `Gift`, stepped to
  `Giving::Done { taken, detail }`. `Files::fetch(name, into, have, progress)` and
  `start_fetch` -> `Fetching`, stepped as a push is, checking the digest over all of the file,
  what was here before included. `Files::offers()`. A client's fetch numbers have the top bit set,
  and the comm core's start from one, so both ways on one session are told apart.
- `docs/client.md`, `docs/comm.md`, `docs/wire.md`.

D16b follows: the index convention and its helper, a fetch held to the index's bound, and
`orion file offers` / `orion file fetch`. C30 (the comm core's own `orion/`) and C31 (configured
offers as the fallback) build on this.

## Proved by

`orion-file-offers` tests, against a comm core on a socket of its own:

- `a_file_offered_on_the_board_is_fetched_whole_and_checked`: 600 KiB, more than a window, so
  the client's answers pace the program; the bytes and the digest are the file's, progress is
  told as it goes, and the program is told who asked and that the client has it.
- `a_fetch_that_broke_off_goes_on_from_what_is_here`: with 70,000 bytes here, the program is
  asked from there, and the whole file checks.
- `a_name_nothing_offers_and_one_the_program_lacks_are_refused_by_name`: `not found`, "nothing
  offers other/x"; and the program's own words for a name it lacks.
- `a_second_program_offering_a_prefix_is_refused_naming_the_holder_and_counted`: busy, "logs/ is
  offered by logger (local:…)"; `offers()` lists the holder and one clash; a prefix that is not one
  is refused; once the holder's session ends, the other takes the prefix.
- `the_longest_prefix_offered_is_handed_the_fetch`: `logs/` and `logs/kernel/` both offered.
- `a_file_whose_bytes_are_not_its_digest_is_not_taken`: rejected on both sides, saying so.
- `a_fetch_whose_program_goes_is_rejected_to_its_client`: "the program that offers it went".

30 of 30 runs pass, and 30 of 30 with every allowed core kept busy.

Not vacuous, each failing the test named:

- the shortest prefix chosen: the longest-prefix test;
- a clash not counted: the clash test;
- the chunk not sized to the client's outbox: the whole-file test and the program-gone test
  (the client is closed for a full outbox);
- the digest not checked: the digest test;
- the program giving from byte zero whatever `from` said: the resume test;
- the client not told its program went: the program-gone test.

## Size

The board's `orion` (aarch64) grew 26,360 bytes as first built, 8,344 past its limit. Two cuts
brought it to 5,237,552, under the 5,240,000 limit with no raise:

- both sides keep their fetches in a list, not a tree (at most 256, so a search costs nothing
  that matters), which drops the tree's code;
- a `Files` hears the fetch and offer kinds only once its program offers, fetches or lists, so a
  program that does none of them, `orion` itself until D16b, carries none of that code.

D16b's `orion file fetch` brings the client side back into `orion`, and has to find its bytes.

## A flake met on the way

The first gate of this tree failed once in `orion-shell`'s
`with_a_login_helper_the_helper_starts_the_shell`, which D16 does not touch. It did not happen
again in 90 runs, quiet and under full load. It is an open entry in `docs/flakes.md`, and the test
now says what it heard in place of `Opened`, so the next occurrence names its cause.
