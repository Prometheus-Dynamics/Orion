## Build

The pins move from `b0a75d93` to `e9224a53`, which brings three changes in the device library:

- **M34.** An entry may name no buffer: a job that ended with nothing to show.
- **R1a.** A publisher can cut a reader off by hand.
- **R2b.** A held buffer's bytes can be read with a check that the buffer was not reused under the
  read.

Orion passes each on to its programs. The bridge and the other consumers take this Orion rev with
that device-library rev together.

## Built

- **The pins.** All `shm-*` workspace dependencies are at
  `e9224a5378f772bab4c4beee3703beee9dd8a166`. The two example lock files follow for
  `shm-sample-time`. Only `shm-ring-buffers` and `shm-ring-holds` changed between the revs:
  `shm-ring`, `-time` and `-doorbell` are unchanged. The shared-rings hash (`0x81a5753a1c9576e6`)
  and the layouts (2, 2, 2) are the same.
- **`NextBatch::Batch` is now `NextBatch::Frame`**, and there is a new arm,
  `NextBatch::NoBuffers` (`seq`, `time`, `meta`), for an entry that names no buffer. Orion
  re-exports `NextBatch`, so a subscriber matches the new arm directly. It is never a frame with
  no buffers. Every match in the workspace is updated.
- **`orion-buffers`:**
  - `BufferPublisher::publish_empty(time, meta)` publishes a job's end with nothing to show. It
    takes no buffer, so it is never `Dropped`.
  - `BufferPublisher::holders_of(buffer)` and `BufferPublisher::cut_off(slot)` cut a subscriber off
    by hand. Its buffers are reusable at once, and its next read is `NextBatch::CutOff`.
  - `AdoptedBuffers::cut_off(slot, pool, table)` does the same for an adopted topic, for Orion's
    own slots only. A program's own reader is `false` and is never touched.
  - An adopted topic's zero-buffer entries reach Orion's subscribers as they are. There is nothing
    to hold.
  - `Torn` and `NoBuffers` are re-exported.
- **Readers in the workspace now read through the checked `Held::read` or `Held::current`:** the
  drills' viewer, ring-bench and the buffer tests.
  - The viewer prints `reused N` for a value dropped as `Torn`. It still prints `torn N` for bytes
    that are not the frame's without the check catching it, which is the failure the drills look
    for.
  - `Held::bytes` is documented as unchecked.
- **The reason for a job's end.** The metadata of an entry with no buffer is the publisher's own
  words, as a frame's is. Orion does not decode a reason from it: that is the frame format's, on
  the device library's side.
- **Nothing on the wire changes.** No version is bumped.

## Proved by

`crates/client/buffers/tests/ended.rs`:

- `a_job_that_ended_with_nothing_to_show_reaches_a_subscriber_as_its_own_arm`:
  - The publisher sends a frame, then an entry with no buffer, then a frame.
  - The subscriber sees `Frame`, then `NoBuffers` with the time and metadata, then `Frame`.
  - Both buffers are free afterwards.
- `a_subscriber_cut_off_by_hand_gives_its_buffer_back_and_a_late_read_is_dropped`:
  - While one buffer is held and the other carries the newest frame, `take` fails.
  - `holders_of` names the holder. `cut_off` returns `true` once, then `false`.
  - The held buffer is published again at once.
  - The subscriber's late `Held::read` gives `Err(Torn { buffer })`, and its next read is
    `CutOff`.
- `an_adopted_topic_cuts_off_only_orions_own_subscribers`:
  - The program's `publish_empty` reaches Orion's subscriber as `NoBuffers`.
  - `cut_off` of the program's slot 0 returns `false` and leaves the slot untouched.
  - Orion's slot is cut off, and its subscriber reads `CutOff`.

Mutations, each killed by its test:

- `publish_empty` publishes nothing.
- `cut_off` does nothing.
- `holders_of` names nobody.
- The adopted `cut_off` loses its own-slot guard.

`cargo tree --workspace --all-features -e normal,build,dev` shows each `shm-*` crate once, at
`e9224a53`.
