## Built

- `orion-time`: `TimeSync::start(comm, topics, config, state_dir)`. SNTP client and server (UDP,
  and the same 48 bytes over TCP), the rules from dev unchanged (slew under 0.4 s, step only
  after two agreeing samples, never step backwards once synchronized, wrong sources passed over,
  saved time restored at start, the synced flag file), `report` and `discipline` modes.
- Settings come from `orion-config`: `time.sync`, `time.servers`, `time.serve`,
  `time.serve_authority`, `time.poll_min_ms`, `time.poll_max_ms`, `time.synced_file`.
- State is the topic `orion.time`, published when it changes. Dev's time-to-live of three polls
  is gone with the status keys it belonged to.
- The operator source is reworked for the session wire. Dev had the operator drive two signed
  requests with a token. Here the operator sends `TimeOffer` once (it needs the grant
  `clock.set`), and the board asks with `TimeRequest` on that session whenever it polls, measuring
  the round trip on its own monotonic clock. Anyone may send `TimeRequest` to compare clocks.
  Two kinds were added, `TIME_OFFER` 0x72 and `TIME_TURN` 0x73; no recorded vector changed.
- The kernel calls (`clock_settime`, `adjtimex`) are in `orion-measure`, feature `clock`, because
  `unsafe` is not allowed in `crates/comm/time`. `LocalPublisher::declare` moved into the broker
  so this crate and `orion-self-status` share it.

## Proved by

Dev's tests carried over (`src/tests.rs`, `src/sntp.rs`, `src/discipline.rs`), against real
loopback sockets and a clock that records what it is asked. New in `tests/time.rs`: a board
takes a serving board's clock and the topic says so; the TCP path; an operator with the grant
steps the board's clock after two agreeing answers, and one without it, or a local program, is
refused.

## Not here

The operator's side of the exchange (answering `TimeRequest` after an offer) belongs to the
client library, group D. Nothing was run on a board: the kernel calls are the ones dev ran.
