## Build

- Declare a pool, publish frames; subscribe with a request and hold or release frames.

## Proof

Two-process frame loop with no copy.

## Built

- `orion-buffers`: `Buffers::declare(name, history, max_meta, pool)` gives a `BufferPublisher`
  (`take`, `buffer_mut`, `publish`, `poll`, `replace_pool`); `Buffers::subscribe(topic, request,
  limits)` gives a `BufferSubscriber` (`next`, `wait`, `held`). A frame's `Held` keeps its
  buffers out of the publisher's hands until it is dropped.
- The publisher offers its pool once, takes in each subscriber's page of hold marks, and only
  then answers that it is honouring them, so a subscriber never reads a frame that is not yet
  protected. A subscriber over its limits is cut off in `take`.
- Buffer topics are for sessions on the board's own socket. Anywhere else the calls say so.
- `orion-topics` gained what this needs: `subscribe_holding`, `Publisher::take_writer` and
  `wake`, `Subscriber::take_reader` and `wait_until`. `BufferReader` gained `pending`.

## Proved by

`tests/frames.rs`: this test is the comm core and the camera, and starts itself again as the
viewer in another process. 400 frames of 1 MiB each: every one read by the viewer where the
camera wrote it, checked across the whole megabyte, with each buffer at the same address every
time it is used. 0 allocations per frame on both sides after the first fifty, no frame dropped
with the viewer holding two of eight buffers, and the comm core handled fewer than 40 requests
for 400 MB of frames. Ran clean seven times in a row.

## Not here

- A ring replaced under a subscriber (the topic declared again differently) is not followed
  for buffer topics; a plain topic's is.
- Dev's reading list was the Styx client. Nothing from it was needed: the ring and the marks
  are B8 and C5.
