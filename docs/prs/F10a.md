## Build

The far end of a link was always the link's device. A tool on a USB cable or a serial port
could not prove a key, so with security on it could never be an operator there: no shell over
the cable, in recovery or out of it, unless it went over the network instead. F10a puts the
signed handshake of `orion+tcp` on links, the first piece of F10 (reaching a node through
another), and the proof includes a shell over a link with security on.

1. **Link kinds**, `0x90` to `0x9F`: `LinkSignHello`, `LinkSignChallenge`, `LinkSignProve`,
   `LinkSignAnswer`, `LinkSealed`.
2. **The board's side** in the link carrier: the same values and signatures as the network
   handshake (`orion-net-handshake`), a principal from the trust store, frames sealed after.
3. **The client's side**: a session over a link with a key is signed; `serial:...#<key>` pins
   the board's key.
4. **Recovery**: its links take signed sessions, and its trust store holds the recovery keys,
   granted `login`.

## Built

As above.

- **The board's side**, `crates/comm/link-serial/src/signed.rs`. A first frame of kind
  `LinkSignHello` is answered by the carrier before the server sees anything: the board's
  fresh values and signature, then the tool's proof. A proof that verifies with a key the store
  has not revoked makes the connection that key's (`Operator`, or `Peer` if pinned), offering
  `CLIENT` and `PEER` as the network does; anything else gets `LinkSignAnswer` refused and the
  link waits for its next frame. 5 seconds for the handshake. After it, each frame both ways
  is a `LinkSealed` with the frame and its MAC (the network's `Seal`: per direction, counted),
  so a frame changed, added, dropped or played back fails, and ends the session, not the link.
  `StreamCarrier::signed(keys)` turns it on; without it a tool is told the link takes no
  signed sessions.
- **The comm core** gives its serial and USB links the trust store. Links that cross the
  network (`links.sealed`) stay as they are: what comes through them is a device's traffic.
- **The client**, `crates/client/connect/src/link_sign.rs`. With `Options::identity`,
  `Session::over_link` and a `serial:` address do the handshake before hello; the frames are
  then 32 bytes shorter (`LINK_SEALED_OVERHEAD`). `serial:<path>[@baud]#<key>` pins the board's
  key (`ClientError::OtherKey` as on the network); a pinned address without a key is refused
  before the port is opened. Without a key nothing changes: the client is the device.
- **Recovery** keeps the recovery keys operators of its in-memory trust store, granted `login`,
  read again every 2 seconds; a key taken out of the file loses its grants. Its USB links, named
  or any name root offers, take signed sessions.

- The wire vectors' samples of the shell's kinds and the link's moved to
  `samples/shell_and_link.rs`, to keep `samples.rs` under 800 lines; the vectors are the same.

## Proved by

- `crates/comm/comm-app/tests/signed_link.rs`,
  `a_tool_with_a_key_gets_a_shell_over_a_usb_link_with_security_on_and_in_recovery`: a board
  with security on and a USB link handed over as the gadget's owner does.
  - Without a key: the device, refused a shell (`denied`).
  - With a key, enrolled over the link itself with the enrollment key (`operators.actions =
    login`): a shell as this account.
  - Again, already enrolled: a shell.
  - A key granted nothing: refused.
  - In recovery from the record of the last good start, with the key in `recovery.keys`: a
    shell; another key refused.
  - Not vacuous: with recovery's link not signed, the recovery case fails ("this link takes no
    signed sessions"); with a signed session made the device again, the operator's shell is
    refused.
  - Run 10 times with the shell and recovery tests, no failure.
- `crates/client/connect/tests/connect.rs`, `a_tool_with_a_key_signs_its_session_on_a_link`:
  every call of the other addresses over a signed link on a pty, with no allocation in 500
  requests; a revoked key told so; a plain session (the device) on the same link after it, and
  signed again after that. `a_serial_address_names_the_key_its_board_must_prove`.
- `crates/comm/link-serial`, `signed::tests`: a sealed frame opens once and in order; one
  changed (with its outer check made good again), dropped, played back or sent plain does not.
- `crates/testing/wire-vectors`: the five bodies.

## Different from the brief

The F10 design lettered the pieces in table order, channels first. The coordinator moved the
signed handshake ahead, as F10a, because a shell over a link with security on needs it now;
channels on a link follow as their own piece. No separate brief was written before the work.
