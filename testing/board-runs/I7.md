# I7: The proof on hardware, on a CM5

Every row of "Proof on hardware" in `docs/design.md`, on a CM5 (Raze #2, image dev.993, kernel
7.2.9), with the board lock held. `next` is at I6 (1fa0ca7) plus this PR. `orion`, `orion-drill` and
`publish-video` were built for `aarch64-unknown-linux-musl` (profile `service`; the example in
release) and run from `/run`; everything was removed afterwards, and the board's own Orion
service was not touched.

## Orion while programs exchange values and frames

`orion-drill` programs, each its own process, around the comm core and node logic: a
publisher counting at 1 kHz with a subscriber, and a camera of 4 KiB frames at 100 a second
with a viewer, all on the board. Over ten seconds the subscriber read 9,506 values and the
viewer 996 frames; the comm core used 3 ticks of CPU (30 ms in 10 s, 0.3% of one core) and
node logic none. The publisher alone used 5. Same-board topics go from ring to ring, and
Orion is not in the path; what the comm core did spend is its own housekeeping.

The design names graphs (the Daedalus bridge, I3) for this row; the drill programs are what
the board ran, since `daedalusd` is not built for it. A graph's node reads and writes through
the same client.

## Kill each part while running

The same programs, plus a workload (`sleep`) under the sample executor, each part killed with
`SIGKILL` while the others ran. Times are from `/proc/uptime`, which counts in 10 ms; "under
10 ms" is the first try after starting.

| Killed | What kept working | How it came back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Node logic | In the next second, 975 values and 102 frames. A request for records was refused `unavailable` | Started again, it answered in under 10 ms |
| Comm core | In the next second with no comm core, 926 values and 97 frames. Nothing new got in | Started again, the command line was in in under 10 ms. The publisher, subscriber, camera and viewer each said `reconnected` once; a viewer that came after got frames (the camera's pool was offered again) |
| The executor | Everything else. Its command went with it, and its workload was marked `Lost: its executor went away` | Started again, the workload ran again |
| Both services | The workload kept running | Started again, the workload was taken back by name and not started a second time: two starts in all, the second still alive |

Through all of it the subscriber saw no gap and the viewer no torn frame. The node logic row
of the design said requests answer "restarting"; they answer `unavailable`, which is what the
relay says for node logic's kinds while it is away; the table now says so.

## Browser

The comm core with its WebSocket on the board's loopback, the video example's clip published
on the board at 30 a second (`publish-video`, JPEG and H.264), and the board's own publisher
and subscriber of the 1 kHz counter beside it. In front of the WebSocket, on the board, a slow
link of 48 KiB a second with small buffers (`orion-drill throttle`): about a third of what
is published. The bench reached the slow link through an SSH tunnel and opened the video
page in a headless browser (`video_from_a_board_plays_over_a_throttled_link`, in
`crates/testing/web-tests`, with `ORION_WEB_BOARD`).

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Pictures shown, at their size | 184 JPEG frames, 160 pixels wide |
| Video plays, on plain HTTP | 184 H.264 frames decoded, 160 wide, the play head at 18.4 s |
| The board sent the slow page less than it published | 92 JPEG frames and 96 H.264 frames were never sent to it |
| After a loss the page got a key frame and played on | Loss at frame 160, 24 more decoded after it |
| The slow page hurt nobody else | The board's subscriber: 56,901 values in order, no gap, no reconnect |
| What it cost | The comm core: 20 ticks of CPU in 60 s |

The first try put the slow link on the bench, behind the tunnel: the tunnel's own buffer (SSH
holds up to 2 MB in flight) then took everything the board sent, the board never saw a slow
reader, and the page got every frame, late. The slow link has to be the first thing the board
writes to.

Live values in a browser are `the_web_client_subscribes_publishes_and_runs_actions_in_a_browser`
(the web client subscribing, publishing and running actions), run on the bench in the gate,
not against the board.

## NT4 as a server, against the SystemCore

The comm core served NT4 on the board's port 5811, publishing the drill counter as
`/OrionI7/level` (double) and taking `/OrionI7/setpoint` (double) as `panel.setpoint`. On the
SystemCore, a client built on WPILib's own NetworkTables (the SystemCore's `ntcore.jar` and
libraries, Java 25) connected to the board, read `/OrionI7/level` for four seconds while
writing a setpoint every 100 ms, and left.

- Connected at once: `NT: CONNECTED NT4 to 172.31.209.217 port 5811`, protocol 4.1.
- The topic came with its type: `double`. 40 values were read in four seconds, the last
  5,804: the counter, at the rate the client asked for.
- All 40 setpoints, 0.5 to 20, were published on the board, in order.
- The comm core used 6 ticks of CPU in 12 seconds.

Nothing was installed on the SystemCore: the client ran from `/tmp` with the jars already
there, and was removed.

The client form is F8 (`testing/board-runs/F8.md`): the board joined the SystemCore's own
robot program as a client.

## Not shown

- **Recovery**: G6 ran the comm core alone in a root holding only it and a shell, and SSH
  went in; a boot into the initramfs needs an image with `orion-recovery` in it, built by the
  image's repository, and a reboot of a shared board.
- **MCU**: the device's size gate (`scripts/device-size.sh`) holds in the gate. A real device
  board against the gateway is H8's run, waiting for an STM32G4 board.
